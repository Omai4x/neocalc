01 — The arithmetic

Type 2 + 3 × 4 into whatever you have now.

A calculator that folds left to right is not broken exactly, it is just answering a different question than the one written on its keys. The trouble starts when the same app has a text field somewhere that does respect precedence, and the two disagree about the same input. NeoCalc had that bug. It was found by writing a test that asked the keypad and the parser the same question.

Left-to-right calculators(2 + 3) then × 4
20
NeoCalc3 × 4 first, as written
14

When you did mean twenty, the brackets are on the main keypad rather than hidden behind a scientific toggle, and a counter in the status bar tells you how many are still open. Tap it to close one.

Also fixed while looking Powers are right-associative, so 2^3^2 is 512 and not 64. Equals closes any brackets you left open instead of refusing. Rotating a piece against the wall nudges it in rather than dropping the input. Small things, but they are the difference between a tool and a toy.
02 — Money

Rates that survive losing signal.

Every rate the app fetches is written to disk the moment it arrives. Open the converter on a plane and it answers immediately from that cache, and says plainly how old the numbers are rather than presenting two-day-old figures as live. The worst possible moment for a converter to be useless is exactly when you are abroad.

Currencies are searchable by code, name or country, so pound finds sterling, the Egyptian pound and six others; yen and NGN both work. Pin the ones you use and they sit at the top of the list and on a board under your result, converted at the same time from one request.

  • 162 currencies

    The full ISO 4217 set the providers quote, each with its country flag, plus Bitcoin, Ethereum, four other coins and gold.

  • A 30-day trend

    A sparkline under every pair, with the change as a percentage and the window's high and low. Enough to answer "is this a good time", not enough to pretend it is a trading app.

  • Alerts that stop

    Watch a pair and get told once, on the crossing. It re-arms only when the rate comes back the other side of your target, so it never nags.

  • Plain words

    "300 dollars in naira". "20% off 45". "15 miles in km". A hand-written matcher rather than anything clever, which is why it is instant and works offline.

03 — Splitting

Somebody has to pay the extra penny.

A hundred split three ways is 33.33 each, which comes to 99.99. Most apps round and quietly lose the penny, or round up and quietly invent one. NeoCalc gives it to somebody and tells you: two people pay 33.33, one pays 33.34, and the shares always add back to the total exactly.

Tip and tax can be applied in either order, there is a round-up mode for when everyone is paying cash, and if you would rather split by what each person actually ordered, tax and tip are shared in proportion.

Why this is worth caring about It is the most common thing anyone does with a calculator at a table, and almost no calculator does it. The arithmetic is trivial; the part that needs attention is that money has a smallest unit, and pretending otherwise is how a split stops adding up.
04 — What it collects

Nothing. Not a softened version of that.

There is no analytics library in the project, no advertising SDK, no crash reporting and no account. You can check that claim rather than take it, because the source is public. Below is every permission the app declares and what it is for.

PermissionUsed forWhen you are asked
InternetFetching exchange ratesNever asked; rates simply need a connection
Network stateDeciding whether a fetch is worth attemptingNever asked
NotificationsDelivering a rate alert you set upOnly after you create your first alert
CameraReading a price off a photo, on the deviceOnly when you tap the camera button

Cloud backup is switched off for this app's data, so none of it reaches Google Drive. Moving to a new phone with Android's direct transfer does copy it, handset to handset, and you can export everything yourself to a file at any time. Full text: privacy policy.

Every build is attached to its GitHub release, tagged to the commit it was built from, and signed with schemes v1, v2 and v3. Your phone checks that signature before installing, which is a stronger guarantee than a hash you would have to compare by eye.

07 — How it is built

Kotlin, Compose, and as few dependencies as the job allows.

Live rates cost the project no library at all: the requests are HttpURLConnection and the parsing is the platform's own org.json. Three libraries beyond Compose earn their place, and each is there because the platform genuinely does not offer the capability.

The palette is orange on warm stone, chosen because operator keys are the only ones that change what the next digit means. Every foreground and background pair in the app was measured against WCAG AA rather than eyeballed; two failed on the first pass and were changed. The reasoning is written down in design-system/neocalc/MASTER.md.

08 — Questions

The things people reasonably ask.

Why is it not on the Play Store?

It will be. The signed bundle exists and the listing copy is written; the gap is screenshots taken on a real device, which have to be captured rather than generated. The APK on this page is the same build.

Why does a calculator want my camera?

Only to read a price off a photo when you tap the camera button. Recognition runs on the device with a bundled model, so the photo makes no network call, is never stored and is never uploaded. The permission is requested at the moment you use the feature, not at launch, and the app installs and runs normally on a phone with no camera.

Is the gold price real?

It is a proxy, and the app says so. Gold comes from the price of PAX Gold, a token redeemable for one troy ounce of London Good Delivery gold, so it tracks spot closely. It is not a bullion dealer's quote. Silver is deliberately absent: no keyless source quotes it reliably, and a wrong number is worse than no number.

How accurate are the exchange rates?

They are daily reference rates from free public sources, not dealing prices. They carry no spread, commission or fee, so what your bank actually gives you will be a little worse. Good for knowing roughly what something costs; not for accounting or trading.

Will an update wipe my history?

No. Every release is signed with the same key, so updates install over the top and keep your data. Because cloud backup is switched off on purpose, moving to a new phone is either Android's direct device-to-device transfer or the export file the app can write for you.

What is the arcade I keep hearing about?

Nothing is hidden in the source, so you can find it there in a minute. Finding it on the phone is more fun, and the clue is that a calculator has a keypad and a display you can hold down.

Can I use the code?

Yes, under Apache 2.0. Fork it, ship it, sell it. If you find something wrong with the arithmetic, an issue with the exact key sequence is the most useful thing you can send.

NeoCalc is a personal project, free and open source under Apache 2.0. Built in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose. The device pictures on this page are drawn in CSS rather than screenshotted, so they cannot fall out of step with the app when a colour or a label changes.
